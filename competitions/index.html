Mathematics · 1:1 03

AMC 10 / 12

American Mathematics Competitions · Mathematical Association of America

“Start from the scoring rules — then stop losing the points you already know.”

1

Blank is a strategy

A blank earns 1.5 points and a wrong answer earns nothing. We train a decision rule for every question, so guessing stops costing points.

2

A 75-minute pacing plan

About three minutes a question on average — but not evenly. Fast through the early questions, deliberate in the middle, selective at the end.

3

The hard end of the paper

Strong scores are decided in the last questions — usually combinatorics and number theory. We build the techniques those problems reward.

4

A path past the AMC

Top scorers are invited to the AIME. For students aiming there, the plan is built as a first step toward it.

Essay · 1:1 04

John Locke Global Essay Prize

John Locke Institute · seven subject categories plus a junior prize

“One strong thesis, defended all the way down.”

1

Choose the question carefully

The essay has to take a position and defend it. We narrow the prompts by interest × arguability × how different the answer can be.

2

Original means past the obvious answer

Read the mainstream view first, then find what it misses. That second question is usually where the essay lives.

3

Meet the counterargument head on

The best essays state the strongest objection and answer it. Every outline has a counterargument section before drafting starts.

4

Make 2,000 words count

Within the limit, every paragraph has to serve the thesis. Editing focuses on cutting what doesn’t.

International affairs · 1:1 05

HIR Academic Writing Contest

Harvard International Review · Junior (grades 7–8) and Senior (grades 9–12) divisions

“Write in the voice of the journal, not an opinion column.”

1

Find the under-covered angle

Summaries of the obvious story don’t stand out. We look for an angle only this student is well placed to write.

2

Analysis, not opinion

Data, cases and how the system works. The outline is fixed as situation → causes → implications before drafting.

3

Style is part of the score

Academic tone, accurate citations and the formatting rules. We edit against published HIR articles.

4

Ready to defend it

Finalists may be asked to present and defend their article. Feedback sessions run as questions and answers to prepare for it.

Before you request

Questions people ask first.

01Do tutors write or rewrite the essay?+
No. Tutors help choose the question, plan the argument and give feedback; every sentence that’s submitted is the student’s. Competitions screen for ghost-writing and AI-generated work, and HIR prohibits AI tools for writing the article.
02When should we start?+
For the essay competitions, about two months before the deadline. For AMC, it depends on the student’s math level — three months before the November exam is a common starting point.
03Can a student prepare for two competitions at once?+
Yes. John Locke rewards an academic argument and HIR rewards journal-style policy analysis, so we plan two different topics so each essay is written for its own competition.
04How do lessons and feedback work online?+
Live sessions on a link our team sets up, usually once or twice a week. Written feedback happens in a shared document, comment by comment, and every draft is kept.
05Do parents get updates?+
Yes — the same tutoring reports as all our lessons: what was covered, how the draft or scores are moving, and what comes next.
