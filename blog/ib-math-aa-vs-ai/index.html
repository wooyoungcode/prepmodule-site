IB Math AA vs AI and how to choose HL or SL

Before choosing IB Math, check what your school offers, what your target universities ask for, and where you stand in maths today.

What is the difference between AA and AI?

AA and AI are the two maths subjects in the IB Diploma Programme. Both cover topics such as functions, statistics and calculus. The courses differ in the kinds of mathematical work they emphasise.

Math AA

Analysis and Approaches emphasises working with expressions and functions and explaining why a mathematical argument holds. Start here if you enjoy rearranging equations and reasoning from a graph.

Math AI

Applications and Interpretation puts more emphasis on finding patterns in data, building models and explaining what the results mean in context. It makes use of tools such as graphing calculators.

Try a sample question from each subject. With the same function, an AA question might ask you to rearrange the expression and explain a feature of its graph. An AI question might ask where a model fits the data well enough to use. See which reasoning you can explain more confidently.

How should you choose between HL and SL?

Both AA and AI are offered at Higher Level (HL) and Standard Level (SL). The IB specifies 240 teaching hours for HL and 150 for SL. If you are considering HL maths, look at the weekly workload for your other HL subjects as well, including assignments and exam preparation.

Three checks before choosing your course

  1. What fits your school timetable? Ask your IB coordinator which subjects and levels are available, how placement is decided, which timetable clashes matter, and when you can still change courses.
  2. What do your target universities ask for? Read the official admissions pages for two or three courses of interest and write down the difference between a requirement and a recommendation. In the US, MIT recommends a foundation in calculus. If you are also applying in the UK, check exactly which IB maths subjects and levels each degree accepts. Imperial’s Mathematics and Computer Science BEng, for example, accepts both AA HL and AI HL and prefers AA. Check the requirements for your entry year.
  3. Where do you get stuck now? Look at two or three missed questions on a recent maths test. Did you get stuck manipulating a function, or did interpreting a model take longer than expected? That tells you what to practise before the course starts.

If you are considering engineering and your school offers AA HL, start by checking whether the universities on your list require or recommend it. Then look at your recent work on functions and trigonometry alongside your other HL commitments. If your school cannot offer the course you want, check accepted alternatives with your coordinator and the university before committing to a timetable.
